Pennsylvania does not test private wells for you. DEP, the Department of Health and Penn State Extension all say testing is the owner's job. There are a few ways to get a test for free or at low cost, though, and they are listed below. We read each one on the program's own page on **2026-10-27**. The free programs run on grant money and limited dates, so check the program page before you plan around one.

## 1. Free testing through Penn State Extension, funded by the PA Department of Health

**What it is.** The Department of Health's private well water page says its Environmental Health Capacity (EHC) program is "partnering with Penn State Extension's Drinking Water Program to offer free water testing for private well owners." The Department's PFAS fact sheet (updated September 2025) calls this "limited free private well testing for Pennsylvania residents," funded by the CDC's Environmental Health Capacity program and other outside funding.

**How it has worked.** Penn State Extension's 2025 program report (updated February 16, 2026) says it was the fifth year in a row. Five statewide webinars and eight in-person workshops (Beaver, Centre, Clarion, Cumberland, Indiana, McKean, Mercer and Northampton counties) ran from November 2024 through July 2025. After attending a webinar or workshop, participants got a free test from the Penn State Agricultural Analytical Services Laboratory for **bacteria, nitrate, manganese, lead, copper, arsenic, sodium and barium**. The report says 220 private water supplies were tested that year. It also gives the share that failed each test, for example 44% for total coliform bacteria and 10% for *E. coli*.

**Who has qualified.** The pages we read describe Pennsylvania residents whose household uses a private well, spring or cistern, who register for and attend the session. Only a limited number of households get a test. We did not find a published 2026–27 schedule on the Penn State pages we checked tonight. Do not assume a session is open.

**How to ask.** The Department of Health page says to contact **dehe@pa.gov** or **717-787-3350** "to learn about free private well testing through Penn State Extension." The PFAS fact sheet gives Penn State's lab contact: **814-863-0841** or **aaslab@psu.edu**.

## 2. Penn State Extension "Safe Drinking Water Clinic" workshops (free, screening only)

Penn State Extension's Safe Drinking Water Clinic is an in-person workshop for people on a private well, spring or cistern. The workshop page says the event is offered at no charge, funded by the Master Well Owner Network grant from PA DEP, and that "free water testing is frequently offered to a limited number of households." Preregistration is required.

Know what you get. The page says the clinic may test bacteria, pH, total dissolved solids and nitrate-nitrogen, and that these results "are not official" and "are not from a PA DEP-accredited laboratory." They help you decide whether to get a lab test soon. They are not a lab test. When we checked on 2026-10-27, the page said: "Please check back for future occurrences of this event."

## 4. County health department programs

We list a county only if its own health department page or document describes the service. If your county is not here, we have not verified a program there. That does not mean there isn't one.

**Bucks County Department of Health — $25 bacteria kit.** The county's Residential Water Testing page says it sells water-sampling kits for **$25 each** (cash, check or credit card). The water is tested "only for Bacteriological Contamination (Coliform Organisms)." Kits are sold 8 am–4:30 pm, Monday–Friday. Call Doylestown 215-345-3336 or Quakertown 215-529-7000. The county's sample-procedure handout (ADM-4, rev. 12/2023) says the test is supplied by PA DEP and "provided at cost," and that the PA DEP laboratory in Harrisburg analyzes it. Samples must be returned the same day they are collected. Doylestown accepts them Monday–Thursday before 2 pm and Quakertown Tuesday and Thursday before 1:15 pm. Samples are not accepted on Fridays or two days before a holiday. *Last verified 2026-10-27.*

**Allegheny County Health Department — coliform bacteria kit (fee not stated).** ACHD's Private Well Testing instructions (revised May 2026) describe a well-water kit that "tests for coliform bacteria only." Kits are available Monday–Friday, 8 am–4:30 pm, at ACHD Water Pollution Control/Solid Waste Management, 836 Fulton Street, Pittsburgh (412-578-8040), and the ACHD Public Health Laboratory, 119 Commonwealth Drive, Warrendale (412-578-8070). The sheet also lists two water-authority offices as kit locations: Hampton Shaler Water Authority (412-486-4867) and Richland Township Water Authority (724-443-9100). Samples must be delivered the same day they are collected. Fulton Street accepts them Monday–Wednesday, 8–10:30 am, and the lab Monday–Wednesday, 8 am–noon. The instructions say results are mailed within two weeks. **The document does not state a price,** so ask when you pick up a kit. *Last verified 2026-10-27.*

## 5. Free help that is not a test

- **Penn State's county test summaries.** The Department of Health notes that Penn State's lab publishes all drinking-water test results it has collected since 2007, sorted by county. These summaries show which problems are common near you, so you can pick tests. [Drinking Water Test Summaries](https://agsci.psu.edu/aasl/water-testing/drinking-water-testing/drinking-water-test-summaries).
- **PrivateWellClass.org.** The Department of Health links this free online class for well owners. The class is delivered by email.

## If none of these fit: paying a lab directly

Bacteria-only kits cover just one problem. For nitrate, lead, arsenic and other chemistry you will usually pay a DEP-accredited lab. Penn State's lab publishes its homeowner packages. On 2026-10-27 the WD01 Standard package (total coliform, *E. coli*, pH, total dissolved solids) was $60, and the other packages and fees are on [our cost guide](/guides/how-much-does-well-water-testing-cost/). For other labs, see our [Pennsylvania DEP-accredited lab list](/labs/pennsylvania/) and [what to test for](/guides/what-to-test-well-water-for/).

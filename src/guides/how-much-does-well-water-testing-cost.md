Testing prices vary by lab, state and package. We only publish prices that a lab or agency prints itself, and we say where and when we read them. These are two examples. They are not an average, and prices at other labs will differ. Fees can change without notice, so check the lab before you send a sample.

We do not list mail-order kit prices yet. We could not verify them from a primary source.

## Example 1: Penn State Agricultural Analytical Services Lab (Pennsylvania)

Penn State's lab is accredited by Pennsylvania DEP for drinking-water analysis and sells mail-in packages to homeowners. You get a kit (cooler, bottles, instructions, form) from many county extension offices and send it back by overnight mail with payment. Fees as published on 2026-10-26:

| Package | What's in it | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| WD01 Standard | Total coliform, E. coli, pH, total dissolved solids | $60.00 |
| WD02 Aesthetics / corrosivity | Standard plus hardness, alkalinity, corrosivity index, copper, iron, manganese | $85.00 |
| WD03 Aesthetics / corrosivity plus lead | WD02 plus first-draw and running-water lead (copper also first-draw and running) | $125.00 |
| WD04 Agriculture / septic | Standard plus nitrate-nitrogen | $70.00 |
| WD05 Mining | Standard plus aluminum, iron, manganese, sulfate | $80.00 |
| WD06 Gas / oil drilling | Standard plus barium and chloride | $75.00 |
| WD07 Trace | Standard plus arsenic, barium, cadmium, chromium, copper, lead, nickel, zinc | $130.00 |
| WD08 Extensive | Standard plus hardness, alkalinity, corrosivity, arsenic, barium, copper, iron, lead, manganese, sodium, chloride, sulfate, nitrate-nitrogen | $220.00 |

Single tests, as published: bacteria (total coliform and E. coli) $40; arsenic $30; corrosivity $45; lead $25; hardness $20; fluoride $20; and $15 each for aluminum, barium, chloride, copper, iron, manganese, nitrate-nitrogen, pH, sulfate, total dissolved solids and total suspended solids.

Lab-stated logistics: bacteria samples must reach the lab within 30 hours of sampling, the lab cannot accept bacteria samples on a Friday, and results are typically complete 10–14 business days after the lab receives the sample.

## Example 2: New Hampshire Public Health Laboratories (New Hampshire)

The state lab's submittal form lists these fees. The form says it accepts **cash or check only**, and tells you to make checks payable to "Treasurer State of New Hampshire". Fees as read from the form on 2026-10-26:

| Test | Fee |
|---|---|
| Standard Analysis (total coliform and E. coli, nitrate, nitrite, chloride, sodium, pH, hardness, iron, manganese, lead and copper (stagnant and flushed), fluoride, arsenic, uranium; radon is the add-on in the next row) | $95 |
| Standard Analysis + Radon | $120 |
| NH Well Water Test for Home Buyers (bacteria, nitrate, nitrite, chloride, sodium, pH, hardness, iron, manganese, lead, copper, fluoride, arsenic, uranium, radon) | $100 |
| Basic Analysis (bacteria, nitrate, nitrite, chloride) | $35 |
| Drinking water bacteria (total coliform / E. coli presence–absence) | $17 |
| Total coliform / E. coli most probable number | $35 |
| Radon only | $25 |
| Radiological analysis (gross alpha, uranium, radon) | $100 |
| Arsenic | $17 |
| Fluoride | $15 |
| VOCs (volatile organic chemicals) | $130 |
| Food license (bacteria, nitrate, nitrite) | $32 |

Package contents above are as the form's own testing guide describes them. Bacteria samples must be tested within 30 hours of collection, and the lab does not accept them after noon on Fridays or the day before a holiday.

## Ohio and New York

We have not verified prices for labs in Ohio or New York. Ohio EPA's list and the Westchester County list do not publish fees. We will add fee information only from a lab's own published fee schedule.

## Why the numbers differ so much

A bacteria-only test and a many-analyte package are different products. The Penn State standard package ($60) and the NH standard analysis ($95) do not contain the same tests. Compare what is included, not just the headline price. Our [what-to-test guide](/guides/what-to-test-well-water-for/) lists what agencies recommend.
